A simplified teaching model.
AssumptionsBaseline: 350 gallons a day per home, 200 outdoors (57%) and 150 indoors. The indoor 150 is split using national shares of indoor use (toilets 24%, showers and baths 23%, faucets 19%, clothes washers 17%, leaks 12%, other 6%), not Phoenix measurements. Leaks here are about 5% of all household use; separately, EPA estimates that fixing easily corrected leaks saves about 10% on a water bill. The 70% residential share of municipal use is illustrative. Presets: WaterSense fixtures scales each end use by the ratio of labeled to federal-standard flow (toilets 1.28 vs. 1.6 gal per flush, −20%; showerheads 2.0 vs. 2.5 gpm, −20%; faucets 1.5 vs. 2.2 gpm, −32%) and assumes an ENERGY STAR clothes washer (about −30%); Xeriscape assumes desert landscaping cuts outdoor use by about three-quarters; Fix all leaks sets leaks to zero. Indoor and outdoor savings are not equivalent for the basin: most indoor water returns as wastewater that can be treated and reused, while outdoor water is mostly lost to evaporation and transpiration. This model counts deliveries, not consumption.
